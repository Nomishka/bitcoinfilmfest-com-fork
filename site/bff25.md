      <section class="bff25-film-notes" aria-labelledby="bff25-features">
        <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Recovered editorial details · part one</p>
        <h3 id="bff25-features">Feature films</h3>
        <p>The feature source describes these four titles in detail. The film posters were referenced by the public source, but their image files were not present in the offline BFF25 folder, so this local preview keeps the text record without inventing replacement artwork.</p>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>UNBANKABLE</span><small>Documentary · 67 min · 2024</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Luke Willms</p>
            <blockquote>“The project began as a series of short films showcasing the strength of microfinance initiatives.” <cite>— Luke Willms</cite></blockquote>
            <p>A directorial debut travelling through Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda, Ghana, South Africa, Ethiopia and Zimbabwe. It follows grassroots financial practices, community banking, mobile money and digital lending, with Bitcoin entering the wider question of how people build access and resilience.</p>
          </div>
        </details>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>REVOLUCIÓN BITCOIN</span><small>Documentary · 80 min · 2024</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Juan Pablo Mejía · Juan en Cripto</p>
            <blockquote>“We hope the film opens minds, sparks curiosity, and inspires viewers to explore Bitcoin not just as technology or investment, but as a powerful tool for freedom and hope.” <cite>— Juan en Cripto</cite></blockquote>
            <p>Filmed over three years with around forty voices across Latin America and Spain, from Bitcoin Beach in El Salvador through Colombia, Mexico and Argentina. The source positions it as a documentary about financial empowerment and personal sovereignty rather than price speculation.</p>
          </div>
        </details>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>HOTEL BITCOIN</span><small>Comedy · 100 min · 2024</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Directors:</strong> Manuel Sanabria and Carlos “Pocho” Villaverde</p>
            <blockquote>“In these challenging times we’re living through, we believe that what the world needs most is comedy.” <cite>— producers Bea Bodegas and Paloma Tejero</cite></blockquote>
            <p>Four friends, 4,000 bitcoins and one laptop set off a Spanish comedy of parties, love, crime and mayhem. The BFF25 source calls the screening a chance to bring the film beyond its Spanish-speaking audience.</p>
          </div>
        </details>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>NO MORE INFLATION</span><small>Documentary · 65 min · 2024</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Maiku Tsukai · Bitcoin Shooter</p>
            <blockquote>“Why does everything get more expensive and is it possible to have no more inflation?” <cite>— Maiku Tsukai</cite></blockquote>
            <p>A two-year production connecting more than twenty-five voices across Jamaica, Argentina and Somalia. Its story follows the human cost of monetary debasement, financial awakening and the search for a different future.</p>
          </div>
        </details>
      </section>

      <section class="bff25-film-notes" aria-labelledby="bff25-shorts">
        <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Recovered editorial details · part two</p>
        <h3 id="bff25-shorts">Experimental shorts</h3>
        <p>The short selection was presented as part of the BFF: Blue / New Media, New Stories strand and described the changing relationship between traditional filmmaking and AI.</p>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>SATOSHI: THE CREATION OF BITCOIN</span><small>AI short, fiction · 2 min · 2025</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Arthur Machado</p>
            <blockquote>“In the face of economic turmoil and personal adversity, a visionary group of friends embarks on a revolutionary journey to create Bitcoin.” <cite>— Arthur Machado</cite></blockquote>
            <p>An experimental narrative inspired by Satoshi Nakamoto, moving from Brazil’s 1990s financial crisis to Europe and the imagined trio of visionaries behind Bitcoin.</p>
          </div>
        </details>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>BITCOIN IS THE MYCELIUM OF MONEY</span><small>AI short, fiction · 2 min · 2025</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Satillionaire · inspired by Brandon Quittem</p>
            <blockquote>“Bitcoin, on the other hand, is an organic system similar to an old growth rainforest.” <cite>— Brandon Quittem</cite></blockquote>
            <p>A BBC-style nature-documentary idea about Bitcoin as a decentralised super-organism of interconnected cells, mixing crisp visuals, facts and humour.</p>
          </div>
        </details>

        <details class="bff25-film" open>
          <summary><span>STRANGE CURRENCIES</span><small>Drama, AI fiction · 3 min · 2025+</small></summary>
          <div>
            <p><strong>Director:</strong> Lauren Sieckelmann</p>
            <blockquote>“With less than $500 we were able to turn a script into something visual.” <cite>— Lauren Sieckelmann</cite></blockquote>
            <p>A pilot trailer about Bitcoin miners in the Smoky Mountains, made by Terence Michael, John McManus and Lauren Sieckelmann. The source frames it as a practical example of AI lowering the cost of visual storytelling.</p>
          </div>
        </details>
      </section>

      <p class="bff25-section-label">05 · recovered public copy</p>
      <h2>Before the curtain rose</h2>
      <p class="bff25-band-lede">The following notes preserve the public orientation material that accompanied the 2025 programme. They describe the event as it was presented before BFF’25; they are not current ticketing, travel, sponsor or submission instructions.</p>

      <section class="bff25-ticket-ledger" aria-labelledby="bff25-tickets">
        <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Archive-only ticket tiers</p>
        <h3 id="bff25-tickets">Frame your seat</h3>
        <p>These were the historical BFF’25 offers. They are listed to preserve the page record only. No ticket, VOD or merchandise sale is active here.</p>
        <dl>
          <div><dt>General Admission</dt><dd><strong>€100</strong><span>Access to all screenings, workshops and parties; the archived offer also mentioned VOD access and merchandise.</span></dd></div>
          <div><dt>2x General Admission</dt><dd><strong>€150</strong><span>The historical “friend of our friend” offer for bringing another person.</span></dd></div>
          <div><dt>Remote Supporter</dt><dd><strong>€25</strong><span>The archived remote option mentioned a stream and BFF VOD for people who could not attend in person.</span></dd></div>
          <div><dt>Producer’s Cut</dt><dd><strong>€250</strong><span>The historical VIP tier mentioned a Producers Dinner, extra merchandise and credits after screenings.</span></dd></div>
        </dl>
      </section>

      <div class="bff25-context-grid">
        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-why">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">The invitation</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-why">Why Bitcoin FilmFest?</h3>
          <ul>
            <li>The first large-scale Bitcoin film festival, then in its third year.</li>
            <li>Not another Bitcoin conference: a cultural, community-driven experience with relaxed, purposeful energy.</li>
            <li>A programme celebrating Bitcoin culture, consciousness and connections through documentaries, dramas, features, shorts, animations, AI videos, fiction stories and more.</li>
            <li>Set in the heart of Warsaw at the Palace of Culture and Science, an iconic city landmark once described in the source as “Stalin’s Ziggurat”.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-warsaw">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">The host city</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-warsaw">Why Warsaw?</h3>
          <ul>
            <li>Warsaw was presented as a city with an old soul and a modern spirit, rich in history and culture.</li>
            <li>It was described as a convenient travel hub and wallet-friendly, especially for food.</li>
            <li>The invitation framed Warsaw as ready for Bitcoin’s spark and spirit.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-travel">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Getting there</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-travel">Travel &amp; Commute</h3>
          <ul>
            <li><a href="https://www.lotnisko-chopina.pl/en/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chopin Airport</a> was described as about 15 minutes from the Palace of Culture and Science; <a href="https://en.modlinairport.pl/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Modlin Airport</a> as about an hour away, with buses and taxis available.</li>
            <li>The public copy pointed visitors to Warsaw buses, trams and metro, with single-ride and day-pass tickets, and to the <a href="https://www.wtp.waw.pl/en/public-transport-step-by-step/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">WTP public transport guide</a>.</li>
            <li>It described Uber and Bolt as affordable and recommended the <a href="https://veturilo.waw.pl/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Veturilo</a> bike-sharing app for exploring the city.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-bitcoin-warsaw">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Local orange map</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-bitcoin-warsaw">Bitcoin in Warsaw</h3>
          <ul>
            <li>The archived copy said there were not many BTC-accepting merchants yet and pointed readers to <a href="https://btcmap.org/map#8/52.30198/21.39621" target="_blank" rel="noopener">BTC Map</a>, including Bar Bazaar as a particularly Bitcoin-friendly place.</li>
            <li>It described Warsaw as having more than 80 Bitcoin ATMs and mentioned “bitomat”, “BTM” and “Bitcoin ATM” as search terms.</li>
            <li>The page also mentioned cryptocurrency exchange offices and peer-to-peer exchange support from the event community. These are historical 2025 statements, not current availability claims.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-sponsor">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">Public support</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-sponsor">Why sponsor?</h3>
          <ul>
            <li>To put a brand in the spotlight and extend its merchandise.</li>
            <li>To fund extras, endorse the programme and support the festival’s mission.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>

        <section aria-labelledby="bff25-submit">
          <p class="bff25-subsection-label">2025 creator notes</p>
          <h3 id="bff25-submit">How to submit my film?</h3>
          <p>The archived 2025 page directed filmmakers to a form or to hello@bitcoinfilmfest.com. That submission route is retained as historical context only; this archive does not open a form or invite active submissions.</p>
          <ul>
            <li>It welcomed documentaries, dramas, comedies, 15–30 minute shorts, 60–90 minute features, animations, AI-generated videos, fiction, trailers and teasers.</li>
            <li>Topics named in the source included freedom, Bitcoin, decentralisation, personal and social sovereignty, parallel structures and permissionless culture.</li>
            <li>With limited screening time, an independent jury selected the main programme; selected works became Official Selections nominated for Golden Rabbits. Works not screened in the main programme could still be considered for online, side-event or network presentation.</li>
            <li>The source also presented PoWies as a Bitcoin-only advertising show, separate from film submissions.</li>
          </ul>
        </section>
