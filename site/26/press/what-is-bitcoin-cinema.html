What Is Bitcoin Cinema?

An explainer for anyone who just heard the phrase and raised an eyebrow.

Bitcoin FilmFest · Warsaw

The question comes up before anything else. "A Bitcoin film festival — what's a Bitcoin film?"

The people who run Bitcoin FilmFest hear it constantly, and their answer is plain. There are sports films. There are religious films. There are films about food and war and science. A Bitcoin film is just another of those. The category was already out there. Nobody had gathered it into one room before.

Look closely and it has layers.

The obvious one is films about Bitcoin, and there is no shortage of them. Documentaries about inflation eating savings. Films about self-custody, about people building working economies in fishing villages and townships, about the riddle the whole thing still carries: who was Satoshi Nakamoto? That question alone has spawned a small sub-genre, because real unsolved mysteries are rare and filmmakers cannot leave them alone.

Then there is a quieter layer: films where Bitcoin is in the plot but is not the plot. A romance, a comedy, a crime story where Bitcoin happens to be the thing that gets stolen or inherited or fought over. The film is really about love or family or revenge. Bitcoin sits inside it the way a briefcase of cash sat inside a thriller in 1995. As the technology becomes a normal part of life, it becomes a normal part of the stories.

The third layer is the one people argue about. Films that never say the word "Bitcoin" and were finished long before it existed, but carry its instincts anyway. Self-reliance. A distrust of central control. The urge to walk out of a system that is lying to you. The Matrix. The Truman Show. A Bitcoiner watches those and recognises something. BFF screens them not because they are about Bitcoin but because they are about the same thing Bitcoin is about.

"We don't create the category. We discover it, we shape it, every year."

Around the films sits an industry. Cinema, like payments and banking before it, is slowly wiring itself to Bitcoin: films crowdfunded in sats, made by directors who simply use it, released on Bitcoin-native platforms. The festival has a working vocabulary for the whole tangle. Bitcoin Cinema is the ecosystem. A Bitcoin film is the single work. Bitcoin in cinema is what happens when the technology turns up inside a mainstream movie. None of it is settled.

That is the honest part. This is a young field, loose and argumentative, with no agreed canon and no syllabus, and the team running BFF thinks that is exactly the right state for it to be in. The genres keep multiplying. The tone is anything but uniform; Bitcoin gets celebrated, mocked and doubted, sometimes in the same afternoon. Each year roughly 25 films are submitted and around 15 or 16 make the live programme — a real selection, judged by a jury of filmmakers and Bitcoiners. The strongest films do not push anything. They let you find your own way in.

🤖 The door is opening

What is shifting fastest is who gets to make a film at all. For decades "filmmaker" meant access — to gear, to budgets, to an industry that decided who was allowed. AI is quietly dissolving that. The 2026 festival runs AI workshops where attendees build a short on the spot, and an AI contest, Future of Money, with a 2.5 million sat prize pool. BFF's own read on it is blunt: more small creators, fewer big productions. Not a dilution. A door opening.

Here is the thing an outsider should know. Bitcoin FilmFest is not only a festival. It runs media, a newsletter, a community, a network; it shows up at other events carrying trailers like a travelling envoy; it has staged "minis" in Lisbon, Cape Town, San Salvador and Lugano. The festival is the once-a-year gathering of a scattered family. The thing underneath is a movement, and the people in it have a name for each other that says most of it: BFF best friends.

You do not need to own any Bitcoin to follow a good story. You do not need to understand the technology to feel what a room full of people who care about freedom feels like. That is the way in. A white rabbit, if you want: you follow it because you are curious, and you end up somewhere wider than you expected.

The festival is in June, in Warsaw, inside one of the largest buildings in Europe. But Bitcoin Cinema is not waiting for June. It is already scattered across hard drives and crowdfunding pages and festival laurels, slowly finding its shape. BFF is just where it gets to sit in one dark room for a few days, and find out how much of it there already is.

📍 Bitcoin FilmFest 2026 runs June 4–7 at Kinoteka, Warsaw.
