Fix the Culture: why Bitcoin culture must be open

The festival's motto says: fix the money, fix the culture. But "fixing the culture" also means caring for it inside the Bitcoin world itself — so that it stays open, creative and welcoming to people from different camps.

Bitcoin FilmFest · Warsaw · 2026

"Fix the Money. Fix the Culture." — the Bitcoin FilmFest motto sounds like a call aimed outward, at a world wrestling with inflation and lost trust. But it has a second, less obvious layer. Culture has to be tended inside the Bitcoin world too. And it's this work that the festival treats as its mission.

🎨 Technology isn't everything

Bitcoin adoption won't happen through better wallets, simpler apps and favorable regulation alone. Technology is necessary but not sufficient. Culture is needed too: language, stories, films, music, symbols and shared experiences. They're what turn an abstract idea into something people can identify with.

That's why Bitcoin FilmFest treats Bitcoin as a cultural movement, not merely a technology or a financial instrument. And a healthy culture needs room to create, to talk and to disagree.

🤝 Openness over orthodoxy

The festival deliberately bets on pluralism. The Bitcoin world develops best when ideas compete in fair rivalry — in a battle of ideas and creative work, rather than by excluding those who think differently. We shouldn't stigmatize artists, or anyone else, for having a view of Bitcoin other than the orthodox one.

It's also why this year's edition took place alongside MoneroKon — a conference devoted to privacy and financial technologies. Instead of building walls between communities, the festival chose to build a bridge. The result was a genuinely good synergy: people from both events watched the same films, listened to the same panels and discovered how much they have in common.

"Bitcoiners don't exist only in cyberspace. We're a network of friends."

🧭 Loyalty to principles, not camps

Openness doesn't mean having no backbone. Quite the opposite — it's about loyalty to the principles that truly matter: freedom, decentralization, honesty and open source. These are the things to defend for the sake of Bitcoin's future. But defending principles is different from shutting down debate. You can hold firmly to values while still leaving room for new voices and new perspectives.

What might that look like in five years? A Bitcoin world that is friendly and open to people from different camps — provided we're united by what matters most: the will to fix the money and to cultivate a culture of honesty, independence and openness.

🎬 When film does what a chart can't

The festival is not short of evidence that this approach works. There's the story of one creator — let's call him John — who had almost lost faith in the art world and in the community that receives his work. It was thanks to Bitcoin FilmFest that he regained it and once again wants to create. He found his community.

Stories like this recur year after year. Many attendees say it was film — one of the most powerful media — that changed how they see Bitcoin: mining, the technology, the whole world of it. Because film can reach where a chart or a report cannot.

🌱 What a creator can do

If you're an artist or a creator, the most important thing you can do for Bitcoin culture is simple: share your work with the world. Come to the festival, show it, meet other creators, swap ideas, find your audience and bring your friends. That's how culture grows — not from the top down, but from people.

The next chance: the fifth edition of Bitcoin FilmFest, June 24–27, 2027 at Warsaw's Kinoteka.
