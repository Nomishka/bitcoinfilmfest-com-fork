## Overview

**The Legend of Landi: Requiem for a Floating City** is an upcoming documentary by filmmaker Oswald Horowitz, set for release in Summer 2027. The film chronicles the extraordinary and tragic story of Samuele Landi, a 58-year-old Italian entrepreneur who became the first person to live continuously in international waters for over 13 months while pursuing an audacious dream: building a sovereign floating city-state beyond the reach of any nation's laws.

## The Story

### Samuele Landi: Fugitive and Visionary

Samuele Landi fled Italy in December 2008 to escape fraud charges related to a bankruptcy scandal, for which he was sentenced *in absentia* to eight years in prison. Refusing to accept state authority over his life, Landi engineered an extreme exit: he would live entirely outside national jurisdiction, in international waters.

In December 2022, Landi anchored the Aisland 1—a 72-meter-long, 19-meter-wide barge—approximately 45 kilometers off the coast between Iran and the United Arab Emirates, in the Persian Gulf. On board were five residents: Landi himself, a crew of sailors and a cook, and five cats. Despite the harsh conditions—in January 2023, high waves destroyed their supply boat, leaving the residents to survive on fish for 28 days after their 10-day food reserves were exhausted—Landi persisted in his vision.

### The Seasteading Dream

Landi envisioned the Aisland not merely as a home but as a prototype for an audacious future: the **Aisland Floating City**, a modular, sovereign settlement designed to house 5,000 people in international waters near the Saya de Malha (a submerged ocean bank northeast of Madagascar). The city would be self-governing, free from tax authorities, border control, and regulatory oversight—a libertarian utopia realized through maritime engineering.

This vision aligned with the broader **seasteading movement**, which seeks to establish sovereign floating communities beyond national control. The Seasteading Institute, founded with backing from billionaire Peter Thiel, had long pursued similar ambitions with French Polynesia, though those projects stalled. Landi represented the romantic—and dangerous—vanguard of this frontier.

### The Documentary & Filmmaker

Oswald Horowitz, a filmmaker with a keen eye for libertarian and exit-culture narratives, began documenting Landi's experiment in late 2023. Horowitz visited the Aisland in December 2023, capturing intimate footage of Landi aboard the barge, discussing his philosophy and plans. The filmmaker would later recall Landi's words: "I will die at sea for sure. I'm not going back."

### Tragedy in the Persian Gulf

On February 2, 2024—exactly five years after the first modern seastead (XLII) was established off Thailand—a rogue wave struck the Aisland 1 as it floated near Dubai. The massive wave breached the hull and apparently broke the barge in two. Of the seven people aboard, only two crew members survived, clinging to driftwood until a passing vessel rescued them the next day.

Samuele Landi, along with two employees—Abdul and Javed Khan, Pakistani laborers working to send money home to their families—were lost at sea. Days later, Landi's body washed up on a beach approximately 40 kilometers north of Dubai.

Italian news outlets and the seasteading community struggled to comprehend the loss. Some residents of Arezzo, Landi's hometown, expressed skepticism about his death, citing the absence of DNA evidence and circumstantial details. Nevertheless, the incident marked the tragic end of Landi's lived experiment in radical freedom.

## Themes & Significance

### Liberty vs. Reality

*The Legend of Landi* examines the collision between libertarian ideals and material constraints. Landi's venture embodied the ultimate "exit" strategy: if you reject state authority, simply leave the state. Yet the ocean—indifferent to ideology—imposed consequences no government could have matched. The documentary explores whether true freedom from institutions is possible, or whether human survival itself depends on collective frameworks.

### Seasteading and the Future

The film situates Landi within the broader seasteading movement and the appeal of floating cities as responses to climate change, overpopulation, and political disillusionment. It examines both the utopian promises and the practical dangers that earlier seasteaders had warned about. Captain Patrick von Stieglitz, a veteran seafarer, had advised Landi that his barge was fundamentally unsafe—a warning Landi received but did not heed.

### The Economics of Exit

Landi's escape from Italian legal jurisdiction raises complex questions about economic sovereignty, tax evasion, and the legitimacy of state authority. The documentary contextualizes his story within a global wave of individuals and entrepreneurs seeking to transcend national borders and regulatory regimes.

### Courage and Hubris

Horowitz's portrayal avoids simple judgment. Landi emerges as charismatic, determined, and visionary—yet also stubborn and perhaps reckless. The filmmaker has emphasized that Landi inspired everyone who met him; his death is treated not as vindication of warnings against seasteading but as a tragedy that reflects the genuine costs of pioneering.

## Connection to Bitcoin & Cryptocurrency Culture

While *The Legend of Landi* is not explicitly a Bitcoin film, it resonates deeply with Bitcoin's ideological core: distrust of state monetary and regulatory authority, belief in decentralized governance, and the conviction that individuals should be free to opt out of state systems entirely. Samuele Landi embodied the ultimate expression of this ethos—not just financial sovereignty, but physical and jurisdictional exit.

The film will likely appeal to audiences interested in cryptocurrency, libertarian philosophy, seasteading, and alternative governance models. It complements other films in the Bitcoin FilmFest library that explore themes of freedom, decentralization, and the human cost of pursuing radical autonomy.

## Legacy

In the seasteading community, Samuele Landi is remembered as a heroic figure—the first person to sustain life in international waters for over a year. Yet his death serves as a sobering reminder that the ocean, like all frontiers, exacts a price. The documentary honors both his audacity and the real dangers of his vision.
